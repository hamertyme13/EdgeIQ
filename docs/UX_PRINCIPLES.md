# EdgeIQ UX Principles

1. **Decision first.** Today answers what deserves attention; Research explains a prop; Entry evaluates the combination; Results shows observed performance; Ask EdgeIQ answers a specific question.
2. **Explanation second.** State why a recommendation looks promising and why it can lose with equal clarity. Keep prose short and plain.
3. **Evidence third.** Reveal history, line movement, calibration sample size, and provider freshness on request. Preserve source and timestamp.
4. **Diagnostics last.** Brier score, model versions, snapshot IDs, and raw provider details belong in deeper views, not the first scan line.
5. **Uncertainty stays visible.** Model probability, calibrated probability, market probability, EdgeIQ Score, and grade are distinct signals. Never imply a verified edge when the exact price, payout, or sample is missing.
6. **No silent dead ends.** Loading and empty states say what is happening and suggest the next available action. Errors explain impact and recovery in user language.
7. **One source, many views.** A recommendation should not gain different meaning when reached from Today, Research, or Entry. Preserve offer identity and evidence through navigation.
8. **Accuracy has a declared denominator.** Recommendation Accuracy reports settled Win/Loss decisions from Entry Builder and generated cards with a saved complete-card probability of at least 60% at evaluation. Lower-probability cards remain in the ledger and calibration data; cards without a saved probability are unscored, not presumed to qualify. Paper Accuracy covers all paper confidence buckets.
9. **Intervals are evidence, not promises.** Show the middle half of a forecast only when both percentiles are recorded and valid. A partial calibration adjustment may inform research but must not masquerade as a fully calibrated headline.
